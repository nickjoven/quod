## Convention and scope

This instrument uses H=-g^2 d_theta^2-2 eta cos(theta)/g^2 on the circle,
with normalized Haar measure dtheta/(2pi), and observables P=cos(theta),
P^2=cos(theta)^2. The potential normalization matches the existing SU(2)
development convention. This is a declared development choice, not verified
replication of the missing pilot. No target is selected or executed.

The ten existing development g values at eta=1 are retained. Fourier
cutoffs K=20,40,80,160,320 use modes -K,...,K; periodic grids have
600,1200,2400,4800 nodes. A periodic grid has no interior/end boundary
distinction. These cutoff values are an explicit U(1) development ladder;
they have the same full matrix dimensions as the previous character ladder.

## Fourier representation

In the bilateral Fourier basis, diagonal entries are g^2 n^2 and neighboring
entries are b=-eta/g^2. Reflection separates the even and odd sectors. The
orthonormal cosine basis has modes n>=0 and first hopping sqrt(2)b; later
hoppings are b. The sine basis has n>=1 and hopping b throughout. Both
tridiagonal parity problems are solved separately. The even eigenvectors are
embedded in the bilateral basis for observable multiplication.

P shifts a coefficient one index in each direction with factor 1/2. Applying
P and P^2 retains all additional coefficients before projection. The full
omitted projection Gram matrix and outside-basis eigenpair residuals are
recorded. All finite even eigenvectors are retained for spectral weights.

## Independent periodic-angle representation

The periodic positive second-difference L includes wraparound entries. The
fourth-order kinetic matrix is g^2(L+h^2 L^2/12), with h=2pi/N. The same cosine
potential is diagonal. Reflection-even and reflection-odd orthonormal
embeddings include the fixed points theta=0 and pi only in the even sector.
Their dimensions sum to N and their ranges are orthogonal.

The reduced sparse matrices are solved with SciPy ARPACK shift-invert, using
a shift strictly below -2|eta|/g^2 and a deterministic starting vector. See
the [SciPy eigsh documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.3/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh.html)
for the shift-invert selection and convergence contract. Four low states in
each parity are retained. Their full-grid residuals and parity defects are
recorded. The operator discretization and eigensolver algorithm differ from
the Fourier route, but both still share SciPy and floating-point arithmetic.

## Full gap versus observable threshold

The full gap is min(E_even,1,E_odd,0)-E_even,0. The first even gap is
E_even,1-E_even,0. Both P and P^2 preserve reflection parity, so their
transition amplitudes from the even vacuum to odd states vanish by symmetry.
For interacting development cells the lowest odd state can therefore set the
full gap without setting either observable threshold. Even-state overlaps
still need uncertainty bounds before a threshold is assigned.

The record's first-three-gap array refers explicitly to even excitations;
the full gap and first odd gap are separate fields. The numerical lowest-parity
label is not a certified ordering claim, particularly at free degeneracies.
At eta=0 the analytic Haar moments are 0,1/2,1/2,1/8,0 in the established
five-component ordering, differing from SU(2). Free P couples to even n=1;
centered P^2 couples to even n=2.
